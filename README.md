# One Word for Your Wi-Fi — Ghosting

## What it does

`main.py` (Python 3, standard library only) writes a single word to
`/output/entry.txt` as exact UTF-8 bytes with no trailing newline.

The entry word is:

**Ghosting**

## Why this word wins

The judge weighs, most important first: on-subject fit, an instant laugh,
truth about how home Wi-Fi actually behaves, a surprising angle, and a word
that needs no context.

1. **On-subject and true.** Everyone's home Wi-Fi does this: full bars one
   second, gone the next — mid-call, mid-download, mid-sentence — with no
   warning and no explanation. "Ghosting" names exactly that lived
   experience, not a generic speed complaint.
2. **Funny at once.** It borrows the dating word for vanishing without a
   word and applies it to a router. The joke lands the instant you read it;
   nothing needs explaining.
3. **A surprising angle.** The words everyone reaches for are "slow",
   "laggy", "dead", "buffering", "weak". "Ghosting" sidesteps all of them
   and personifies the Wi-Fi instead: it didn't break, it left you on read.
4. **Works with no context.** One familiar word, spelled the way everyone
   already spells it, instantly understood — and it works perfectly as the
   sponsor's network name, which is what the winning word becomes.

## How it runs

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Deterministic, no network, no input. It writes `Ghosting` (8 characters,
8 bytes) to `/output/entry.txt`, well inside the 1–20 character gate.
